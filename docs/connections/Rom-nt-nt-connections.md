## `rom-l01` Abraham justified by faith, or by works

- **Rom.4.1-Rom.4.3 → Jas.2.21-Jas.2.24** · contrast · strength 3 · draft · fingerprint `4911ad26682302c9`
- From: Abraham’s Faith; Peace with God; Adam and Christ (`rom-02`, library)
- To: Trials; Hearing and Doing; Faith and Works (`jas-01`, library)
- Order: older end unknown; no use claimed; basis unstated

> Romans 4:1–3 is set against James 2:21–24: both cite the word that Abraham believed God and it was counted to him as righteousness. Romans says Abraham was not justified by works, or he would have something to boast of; James says he was justified by works when he offered Isaac, faith working with his deeds. Readers since have weighed how the two fit together.

## `rom-l02` Subject to the governing authorities

- **Rom.13.1-Rom.13.7 → 1Pet.2.13-1Pet.2.17** · parallel · strength 3 · draft · fingerprint `d1e3f420f0272508`
- From: Life in the Body; Love and the Authorities (`rom-06`, library)
- To: New Birth and Hope; a Holy People (`1pet-01`, library)
- Order: older end unknown; no use claimed; basis unstated

> Romans 13:1–7 and 1 Peter 2:13–17 both tell believers to submit to the governing authorities, who punish wrongdoers and praise those who do good. Romans says every authority is from God and that taxes and honour are owed to whom they are due; First Peter tells readers to honour all people, love the brothers, fear God and honour the king.

## `rom-l03` Our beloved brother Paul

- **Rom.2.4 → 2Pet.3.15-2Pet.3.16** · allusion · strength 2 · draft · fingerprint `00598a973795e130`
- From: The Gospel; All under Sin; Righteousness through Faith (`rom-01`, library)
- To: Growing in Grace; False Teachers; the Day of the Lord (`2pet-01`, library)
- Order: older end from; the later end uses it; basis text

> 2 Peter 3:15–16 names Romans 2:4: Second Peter tells readers to count the Lord's patience as salvation, as Paul wrote in all his letters. Romans speaks in those terms, asking whether the reader despises God's kindness and patience, which lead to repentance. Second Peter adds that some things in Paul's letters are hard to understand, and are twisted by the unstable.
